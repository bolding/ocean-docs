Bias Correction of Atmospheric Forcings — User & Technical Guide

1. Purpose

Ocean and coastal models are sensitive to the quality of their atmospheric forcing fields (air temperature, precipitation, radiation, wind). Global Climate Models (GCMs) reproduce the large-scale climate well but contain systematic biases relative to observations — a GCM may be consistently too warm in winter or too dry in summer over a given region. If those biases are left uncorrected they propagate into the ocean model as spurious trends or drifts.

This tool corrects the bias in a GCM's future projections so that the corrected climatology matches an observational reference (e.g. ERA5) over a historical calibration period.

2. Scientific Background

2.1 What is bias correction?

Let:

  • ref: observed reference (ERA5) over the calibration period
  • hist: GCM historical simulation over the same calibration period
  • future: GCM future projection to be corrected

All three are compared per calendar month (January with January, etc.) so that the seasonal cycle of bias is captured rather than a single annual mean correction.

2.2 Three methods

Delta method (delta)

Computes the difference (or ratio) between the observed and modelled climatological mean and adds it to the future:

  • Additive (+, temperature): future_bc = future + (mean(ref) − mean(hist))

  • Multiplicative (*, precipitation): future_bc = future × (mean(ref) / mean(hist))

Strengths: fast, simple, preserves the model's internal variability. Weaknesses: only corrects the mean; variance and extremes are unchanged.

Quantile Mapping (qm)

Transfers the full empirical CDF of hist onto the CDF of ref, then applies the same transfer function to future:

  1. For each quantile q, find the value v_hist(q) in hist and v_ref(q) in ref.
  2. Build a mapping: v_hist → v_ref.
  3. For each future value x, look up its quantile in hist, then apply the mapping.

Strengths: corrects the full distribution (mean, variance, extremes). Weaknesses: distorts long-term trends in the future projection when future values fall outside the historical range. Not recommended for multi-decadal projections.

Quantile Delta Mapping (qdm, recommended)

The method of Cannon et al. (2015). Preserves the GCM's change at each quantile while correcting the distributional shape:

  1. Find the quantile q of each future value within the future CDF — of a 30-year moving window around the value's year (window_years, moved in 10-year steps, step_years), after removing the window's linear trend (as in ISIMIP3BASD, Lange 2019).
  2. Look up the corresponding historical value: v_hist(q).
  3. Look up the corresponding reference value: v_ref(q).
  4. Apply the delta (additive or multiplicative) from hist to ref at that quantile, scaled by the future value:
  • Additive: future_bc = future + (v_ref(q) − v_hist(q))
  • Multiplicative: future_bc = future × (v_ref(q) / v_hist(q))

Correct the whole future period in one run: the windows are taken within the period given (a period shorter than the window is one window, with a warning).

Strengths: preserves the GCM's climate change signal at every quantile; corrects both mean and distribution shape. Weaknesses: more compute-intensive than delta; requires a sufficient sample size per calendar month.

qdm_v1 is the kernel that was called qdm before 2026-11. It ranked future values in the historical CDF (step 1), which makes it quantile mapping: the model change is scaled by roughly σ_ref/σ_hist and per-cell changes are distorted (GFDL-ESM4 ssp126 tas, NSe domain: cell changes −2.1…+1.2 K against the model's −1.3…+0.4 K). Kept only to reproduce old products. Output files record the method in the bias_correction_method attribute.

Reference: Cannon, A.J., Sobie, S.R. and Murdock, T.Q., 2015. Bias correction of GCM precipitation by Quantile Delta Mapping. Journal of Climate, 28(17), pp.6938–6959.

2.3 Temporal disaggregation (optional)

CMIP6 models typically provide output at monthly or daily resolution. Ocean models often require hourly or 3-hourly forcing. The disaggregation step reconstructs the sub-daily signal by adding the ERA5 climatological diurnal cycle to each corrected daily value.

Algorithm (additive, e.g. temperature):

  1. For each ERA5 calibration hour, compute the anomaly from the daily mean: anom(t) = ERA5(t) − daily_mean(ERA5)(day_of(t))

  2. Average anomalies over all years grouped by (day-of-year, hour-of-day) to produce a (365, n_steps) climatological diurnal signal. The day of year is counted on a 365-day calendar (from month and day; 29 February shares 28 February's slot), so a calendar date has the same slot in every year and for models without leap days.

  3. Apply a circular 31-day smoothing window (centred on each DOY) to reduce noise.

  4. For each corrected daily value, repeat it n_steps times and add the climatological anomaly for the matching (DOY, hour): hourly_bc(day, h) = daily_bc(day) + clim_anom(DOY(day), h)

Each day runs from 00:00 to 23:00 whatever the time stamp of the daily value (CMIP6 day data is stamped at 12:00; before 2026-11 the hours were counted from that stamp, shifting the diurnal cycle by 12 hours for every noon-stamped variable). Output is on the real calendar: for models without leap days, 29 February is inserted as the mean of 28 February and 1 March (to match the tidal boundaries).

Daily mean is preserved by construction: the anomalies sum to zero over the day, so averaging the disaggregated hourly values recovers the original corrected daily value exactly.

Multiplicative disaggregation (e.g. shortwave radiation) uses ratios instead of anomalies; the night-time ratio is set to zero.

3. Workflow

ERA5 (ref, hourly or daily)
        │
        ├── CMIP6 historical (hist, monthly Amon)
        │        │
        │        └── regrid hist & future → ERA5 grid
        │
        ├── CMIP6 future / ssp245 (future, monthly Amon)
        │
        └── bias_correct_forcings.py
                │
                ├── [correct]     → {var}_bc_{year}.nc   (daily, corrected)
                └── [disaggregate]→ {var}_bc_disagg_{year}.nc (hourly, corrected)

Steps can be run together or separately with --steps:

# Step 1: bias correction only (fast, ~minutes)
python python/bias_correct_forcings.py --config bc.yaml --steps correct

# Step 2: temporal disaggregation only (reads existing *_bc_*.nc files)
python python/bias_correct_forcings.py --config bc.yaml --steps disaggregate

# Both in one go (default)
python python/bias_correct_forcings.py --config bc.yaml

4. Configuration Reference

The YAML configuration controls every aspect of the run.

4.1 Calibration and future periods

calibration:
  start: "1990-01-01"   # ERA5 and CMIP6 historical must cover this range
  end:   "2014-12-31"   # NOT later than this: CMIP6's `historical` experiment is a
                         # fixed, protocol-bounded 1850-2014 for every model, no
                         # exceptions (Eyring et al. 2016). A later end date doesn't
                         # error -- it silently truncates the CMIP6 (hist) side of
                         # the QDM fit while ERA5 (ref) still uses the full window,
                         # an asymmetric calibration baseline that bakes in a
                         # spurious "model bias" from whatever real trend exists in
                         # the extra ref-only years. Real incident: this file
                         # previously showed "2019-12-31" here, which is exactly
                         # this bug, undetected in production for months.

future:
  start: "2020-01-01"   # CMIP6 SSP scenario data needed from here
  end:   "2099-12-31"

4.2 Spatial domain

domain:
  lon_min: -20.0
  lon_max:  30.0
  lat_min:  45.0
  lat_max:  75.0

All datasets are spatially subsetted before correction. Coordinates may be provided in either −180..180 or 0..360 convention; the tool normalises internally.

4.3 Variables and correction kind

variables:
  - name: tas    # 2-m air temperature
    kind: "+"    # additive: bc = future + (ref_mean − hist_mean at each quantile)
  - name: pr
    kind: "*"    # multiplicative: bc = future × (ref_mean / hist_mean)

kind defaults to "+" for temperature-like names (tas, t2m, uas, vas) and "*" for precipitation-like names (pr, tp, rsds).

4.4 Correction method

correction:
  method: qdm          # 'delta' | 'qm' | 'qdm' | 'qdm_v1' (legacy)
  n_quantiles: 100     # quantile bins for qm/qdm; ignored by delta
  window_years: 30     # qdm: moving window for the future ranks
  step_years: 10       # qdm: window step

Rule of thumb for n_quantiles: use at most one quantile per ~5 calibration samples per calendar month. With 30 years of daily data you have ~900 samples/month; n_quantiles=100 is fine. With monthly data you have only 30 samples; use n_quantiles=20 or switch to delta.

4.5 Reference (ERA5)

reference:
  tas:
    source: local
    path:   "/data/ERA5/tas_{year}.nc"   # {year} is replaced for each year
    variable: t2m                         # variable name inside the file

  rsds:
    source: local
    path:   "/data/ERA5/ssrd_{year}.nc"
    variable: ssrd
    scale_factor: 0.000277778             # J/m² → W/m²  (ERA5 hourly accum ÷ 3600)

Unit conversion (scale_factor): ERA5 radiation variables (ssrd, strd) and precipitation (tp) are stored as hourly accumulations in J/m² and m respectively, whereas CMIP6 provides instantaneous fluxes in W/m² and kg/m²/s. Add scale_factor: 0.000277778 (= 1/3600) to any ERA5 reference or disaggregation block that needs this conversion. The factor is applied to the ERA5 data immediately after loading, before regridding and bias correction. The same key is supported in the disaggregation.variables blocks.

The reference can also be loaded from Copernicus Marine Service:

reference:
  tas:
    source: copernicus
    uri: "copernicus://cmems_obs-sl_glo_phy-ssh_nrt_allsat-l4-duacs-0.25deg_P1D"
    variable: adt

4.6 CMIP6 historical and future

historical:
  tas:
    source: cmip6
    uri: "cmip6://MPI-ESM1-2-HR/historical/tas/Amon/r1i1p1f1"

future_model:
  tas:
    source: cmip6
    uri: "cmip6://MPI-ESM1-2-HR/ssp245/tas/Amon/r1i1p1f1"

CMIP6 data is downloaded from the PANGEO Google Cloud catalog on first use and cached locally. Requires intake, intake-esm, and gcsfs:

conda install -c conda-forge intake intake-esm gcsfs

URI format: cmip6://<source_id>/<experiment_id>/<variable_id>/<table_id>/<member_id>

4.7 Temporal disaggregation

disaggregation:
  enabled: true
  smooth_window: 15    # DOY-smoothing half-width in days (0 to disable)
  clim_years:   5      # use only last N years of calibration for climatology
                       # (reduces memory; 3–5 years is sufficient)
  variables:
    tas:
      era5_path:     "/data/ERA5/hourly/t2m_{year}.nc"
      era5_variable: t2m
    rsds:
      era5_path:     "/data/ERA5/hourly/ssrd_{year}.nc"
      era5_variable: ssrd

Only variables listed under variables: are disaggregated. Others receive only the daily bias-corrected output.

Memory note: the clim_years parameter limits how many years of ERA5 sub-daily data are loaded for building the diurnal climatology. 5 years of hourly ERA5 on a 101×201 grid requires ~7 GB. Loading 30 years would require ~42 GB; 3–5 years is climatologically sufficient.

4.8 Cache

cache:
  enabled: true
  dir: "/data/kb/cache"

CMIP6 data downloaded from PANGEO is cached as compressed NetCDF4. Subsequent runs use the cached file instead of re-downloading (~30 s saved per variable). Cache files are named by a hash of the source URI, domain, and time range.

4.9 Output

output:
  dir:                  "./bc_output/MPI-ESM1-2-HR_ssp245"

  # ── Year-by-year output (default, clim_period: 1) ──────────────────────
  filename_pattern:      "{variable}_bc_{year}.nc"
  disaggregated_pattern: "{variable}_bc_disagg_{year}.nc"

  # ── Multi-year chunk output ──────────────────────────────────────────────
  # clim_period > 1 groups N years into one file instead of one file per year.
  # Windows are aligned to multiples of N from year 0:
  #   clim_period: 10  → 2020–2029, 2030–2039, …
  #   clim_period: 5   → 2020–2024, 2025–2029, …
  # All daily (or sub-daily) timesteps are written; no averaging is applied.
  clim_period:           10     # 1 = year-by-year (default)
  chunk_pattern:         "{variable}_bc_{start}-{end}.nc"
  disagg_chunk_pattern:  "{variable}_bc_disagg_{start}-{end}.nc"

  compress:             true   # zlib level-4

When clim_period: 1 (the default), one file per year is written using the filename_pattern / disaggregated_pattern templates.

When clim_period: N with N > 1, all daily (or sub-daily) timesteps for N years are bundled into one file. Windows are anchored at multiples of N — e.g. clim_period: 10 with a future period of 2020–2099 produces ten files containing 10 years each: 2020-2029.nc, 2030-2039.nc, …, 2090-2099.nc. A partial window at either edge (e.g. if the future period starts in 2022) contains however many years of data are available.

6. Practical Tips

Choosing a correction method

Scenario Recommended method
Quick test or delta-only forcing delta
Correcting a historical reanalysis period qm
Future projections (2020–2100) qdm
Monthly reference data only delta (too few samples for quantile methods)

Calibration period length

A 20–30 year calibration period is standard practice. Shorter periods (< 10 years) can give noisy quantile estimates, especially for extremes.

Reference data resolution

Match the reference to the model's resolution: for daily CMIP6 data use a daily reference (resample: daily, or daily_sum for accumulations).

  • Hourly ERA5 against daily model data: wrong. The corrected daily values get the spread of single hours, and disaggregation then adds the diurnal cycle on top. NSe domain, ERA5 1990–2014: spread +6–14 % for tas (summer highest), +9–11 % for uas/vas, +3 % for psl; 1 %/99 % tails shifted by 1–2 K for tas (single cells up to 7 K). It also needs ~70 GB of memory for tas.
  • Daily ERA5: ~775 samples per calendar month over 25 years — ample for qdm with n_quantiles: 100.
  • Monthly ERA5: only 25–30 samples/month; use delta or reduce n_quantiles to ~20.

Running steps separately

Run --steps correct first to validate the bias correction output, then run --steps disaggregate to add the diurnal cycle. This avoids repeating the expensive CMIP6 download and regridding if disaggregation parameters need adjustment.

Memory usage

Stage Dominant cost Typical peak
Bias correction ERA5 ref per calendar month ~3–5 GB
Disaggregation (clim_years=5) ERA5 hourly anomaly array ~7 GB
Disaggregation (clim_years=30) ERA5 hourly anomaly array ~42 GB

Set clim_years: 3 in the YAML if memory is constrained.

7. Output File Conventions

Each output file contains a single variable with:

  • time: daily timestamps (bias-corrected) or hourly/3-hourly (disaggregated)
  • lat, lon (or whatever coordinate names the reference grid uses)
  • bias_correction_method attribute on the variable
  • disaggregation attribute describing ERA5 source and parameters (if disaggregated)

The output grid matches the reference (ERA5) grid, not the original CMIP6 grid, because CMIP6 data is regridded to the ERA5 grid before correction.

9. Diagnostics — bc diagnostics

bc diagnostics evaluates calibration quality and produces a standard set of plots and statistics without running the bias correction itself. It reads the same YAML as bc correct and is designed to be run after correction to verify the results look sensible before using the corrected files in a simulation.

9.1 What it produces

For each variable × SSP scenario combination, four PNG plots and one text statistics file are written:

File Description
calibration_maps_{scenario}.png Three-panel map: reference mean | CMIP6 raw mean | bias
seasonal_cycle_{scenario}.png Monthly climatology: reference, CMIP6 raw, corrected future per decade
future_trend_{scenario}.png Annual area-mean of corrected future with linear trend
monthly_breakdown_{scenario}.png Heatmap of corrected future monthly means by decade
statistics_{scenario}.txt RMSE, bias, MAE, correlation per calendar month + overall

{scenario} encodes both the SSP identifier and the regridding method, e.g. ssp245_bilinear. This string also appears in every plot title so figures are self-describing.

9.2 Output layout

Diagnostics are not area-specific — they characterise the atmospheric forcing, which is the same input regardless of which ocean domain is modelled. Outputs therefore live at the top level of the analyses tree:

analyses/
└── scenarios/
    ├── plots/
    │   └── <variable>/           e.g. tas, pr, rsds
    │       ├── calibration_maps_ssp245_bilinear.png
    │       ├── seasonal_cycle_ssp245_bilinear.png
    │       ├── future_trend_ssp245_bilinear.png
    │       └── monthly_breakdown_ssp245_bilinear.png
    └── tables/
        └── <variable>/
            └── statistics_ssp245_bilinear.txt

The path is controlled by output.base_dir in the YAML (default ./analyses). Add this key to any existing bc correct config:

output:
  base_dir: ./analyses          # diagnostics go to analyses/scenarios/
  dir:      ./bc_output/…       # corrected NetCDF files (bc correct only)

9.3 Spatial masking for ocean-only reference data

Some forcing variables (e.g. NDEP nitrogen deposition fluxes) provide observations only over water. CMIP6 models produce output everywhere, so a naive area mean of the model would include land cells that the reference does not, making the comparison meaningless.

bc diagnostics automatically derives a water mask from the reference:

ref_valid = ref.notnull().any('time')

All area means — for CMIP6 historical and for the corrected future — are then computed only over cells where ref_valid is True. This applies to every variable, so for standard meteorological variables (ERA5 covers the full domain) it has no effect, while for ocean-only references it ensures the comparison is grid-cell-for-grid-cell.

No config change is needed: the masking is always active.

9.4 Regridding method

The regridding method recorded in regridding.method (default bilinear) determines the CMIP6→ERA5 interpolation and is embedded in all output filenames and plot titles as part of {scenario}. To compare methods:

# Run 1
regridding:
  method: bilinear

# Run 2
regridding:
  method: conservative

The two runs produce separate files (ssp245_bilinear.png, ssp245_conservative.png) so results can be compared side by side.

historical:

10. Custom Analysis Periods — period_label

All validation scripts (and the output directory structure) use a period string as a subdirectory name:

analyses/areas/<Area>/validations/<Exp>/plots/<model>/<period>/<type>/

By default this is auto-generated from year or years in the config:

Config key Period string
year: 2016 2016
years: [2016, 2023] 2016-2023

Adding a custom label

Set period_label to any string that starts with a 4-digit year:

years: [2016, 2023]
period_label: "2016-2023-DJF"      # winter months only

years: [2019, 2021]
period_label: "2019-2021-storm"     # storm-track sub-analysis

The label is used verbatim as the period directory name. The reference year for {year} path templates is taken from years[0] (or year) as normal.

Constraints

  • Must start with YYYY — required so AnalysesLayout.iter_plot_dirs() recognises the directory as a period rather than a model name.
  • No spaces (use - or _ as separators).
  • Keep labels short: they appear in directory paths and report headings.

Typical use case

Run a full-period analysis first, then add a seasonal or event-specific analysis without overwriting the original:

# Full period
years: [2016, 2023]
# → plots/pyGETM/2016-2023/surface/

# Winter sub-analysis (DJF only — add time_filter to subset months)
years: [2016, 2023]
period_label: "2016-2023-DJF"
# → plots/pyGETM/2016-2023-DJF/surface/

Both sets of plots coexist under the same experiment directory and are discovered automatically by the reporter and manage-analyses tools.
